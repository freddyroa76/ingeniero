Technical Methodology Guide

Pipeline 1. CNGA (California Natural Gas Association)

Empirical correlation ideal for transport conditions where simplicity and speed are required.

  • Range: Best for T < 150°F and P < 5000 psig.
  • Pros: Non-iterative, very fast calculation.
  • Cons: Loses accuracy with high impurities (CO2, N2) or near Critical Point.
Standard 2. Standing-Katz (DPR Method)

Dranchuk-Purvis-Robinson (1974). The Industry Standard correlation for general natural gas engineering.

  • Application: General purpose, metering, and custody transfer.
  • Validity: 0.2 < Ppr < 30 and 1.05 < Tpr < 3.0.
  • Accuracy: ~0.5% average deviation from experimental data.
High Pressure 3. Standing-Katz (DAK Method)

Dranchuk-Abou-Kassem (1975). Based on the Starling-Carnahan Equation of State (EOS).

  • Application: High pressure reservoirs (>9000 psig) and sour gas.
  • Feature: fitted to 1,500 data points, offering robust convergence at extreme conditions.
Robust 4. Hall-Yarborough (HY)

Hard-sphere equation of state (1973).

  • Specialty: Excellent for "difficult" convergence regions.
  • Note: All SK methods include Wichert-Aziz corrections for CO2 and N2 impurities.
